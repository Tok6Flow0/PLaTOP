# Reference-led office design

The supplied `public/reference/office-concept.png` is the starting-space visual target. Its older header spelling is superseded by the user's exact name, **PLaTOP**. The application builds the connected office as actual Three.js geometry. Current rail, roster and team portraits are generated from those same models, not unrelated clipped artwork.

The connected three-by-two campus has terracotta, sage, navy and forest-green room walls, wooden tables, woven rugs, bookshelves, individual plant leaves, paper and laptop props, a front entry and two benches. The central Lantern Desk office opens into the shared foyer. Front-facing wall roots fade with the camera so interiors stay readable. Original team portraits remain available alongside the revised concept-led identities.

Desktop chrome follows the reference's roughly 16.2% left roster and 19% right inspector, cream background, dark pine typography and muted green controls. Small windows use a horizontal team list and stack the inspector under the scene. Camera pan, perspective orbit/tilt, anchored zoom and keyboard equivalents share a bounded world, with an exact reset to the starting composition.

Warm hemisphere/key lighting, a 2048-square static soft-shadow map, contact pads and local lamp glows provide depth. The contact-shading pass reuses the scene colour draw's depth, with eight-sample half-resolution GTAO capped at 800 pixels on its longest side and a 1600-pixel colour target. Transparent walls use coverage-based tone mapping; the CSS-matched background remains untonemapped. Unsupported or failed GPU allocations latch direct output for that scene. This is rasterization, not ray tracing. Rounded woven rugs, rolled clay pots, connected cupped foliage and softly grained timber reduce the isolated-block appearance. Active and sleeping animation use a display-aligned cinematic 30 fps cadence. Coalesced camera input can redraw at up to 60 fps; hidden, minimized, paused and reduced-motion states do not schedule continuous world animation. The scene pixel-ratio cap is 1.5 while interface text keeps native density. Native frame pacing and device power have not been benchmarked.

The user's stricter character-level comparison makes the approved office concept the default model-form reference: cream ceramic helmets, potbellied helpers, soft ivory/navy owls, a green lantern helper, an amber lantern and a small ochre bird. Names, stable IDs and roles remain intact; earlier book, cape and origami interpretations are not the current defaults. Smooth curved surfaces have fitted normals, inset rounded visors, joined feet and fitted belly panels. Each model stays inside its certified motion clearance and below 9,000 triangles. Team portraits render those actual models with a softly defocused botanical studio, baked shadows and lossless PNG compression; they add no live portrait lights or shadow passes.

Agents may choose palettes, task-lamp and independent ambient lighting, and compose connected objects from eleven shapes and seven finishes, with up to 24 parts per slot. These are drawing tools and bounded physical envelopes, not mandatory themes. Wall recipes mount flush to real walls and share their camera cutaways; the open Lantern Desk uses its low parapet. The skill's optional design guidance asks the agent to inspect and refine its actual render without prescribing taste or inventing work. Onlookers still have viewing/playback controls only.

Visual approval is feature-specific. `public/assets/approved.json` enables the reviewed authored models; unapproved deliveries remain available as source but do not automatically replace the reference models. The independent critic's comparison and numbered observations are local artifacts under ignored `art-direction/critique/`. Subjective resemblance scores do not establish pixel-perfect equivalence or approve unreviewed features.

Activity is reported through a local bridge. The current skill publishes bounded native observations and command heartbeats; it does not install an automatic background native chat monitor. Mapped agents carry their adopted identities from their own chats and choose understated actions during real work. Computer, whiteboard, cabinet and lounge gestures are optional expressions, not task categories or a scripted routine. Heartbeats never choose movement or a pose; only an explicit `rest` chooses sleeping. Current confirmed actions can move while activity is unknown, but stale, unknown or idle reports quiet ongoing interaction gestures. Offline scenes preserve position and presence; initial load restores the last choice without replaying a journey or proving an earlier arrival. Decorations remain on this device and do not imply a business milestone or task completion.
